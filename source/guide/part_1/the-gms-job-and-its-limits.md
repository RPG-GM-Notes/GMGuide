<!-- (dl (# What I Take Responsibility For)) -->

I technically have authority over the forces and the world's reactions, but I don't think of myself as standing above the table. I think of myself as its steward. My job is to help create and maintain a situation the table can enjoy, and to respond to what happens without forcing the game down a predetermined path.

That includes making calls in the moment. I use the forces, their goals and limits, and what has already happened to make the world's responses feel like they belong. I also take responsibility for making the game safe enough for everyone at the table to participate.

<!-- (dl (# What I Leave to the Players)) -->

I don't try to predict what the characters will do or require them to follow a planned sequence. The players decide what their characters attempt, and those choices can change the campaign. If a character's action is vetoed for safety, I work with that player to find a different action rather than leaving them without a way forward.

<!-- (dl (# What We Decide Together)) -->

Safety is the ultimate authority at my table. As a group, we decide what can initially go into the game and when it can happen. During play, every player has veto power over content, no questions asked. This is my one hard rule.

When someone uses a veto, the offending context is removed. I make a note, and after the session the only question I ask is whether I recorded it correctly so it can be added to our banned-topics list. The veto does not require explanation or discussion in the moment.

The table's fun is shared, too. I am a member of the table, and my enjoyment matters as much as any one player's. I find enjoyment in doing something well, so I'm willing to throw out the plot, characters, NPCs, or even the system if that is what the table needs. I recommend that prospective GMs figure out what makes GMing enjoyable for them and find a group that wants that kind of game. I usually ask my players to help us find our fun together. If we can't, I end the game; if one person at the table isn't having fun, it tends to tank the whole table.

<!-- (dl (# How Authority Works at My Table)) -->

My authority over the forces and the world's reactions is practical, not absolute. I use it in service of the table, and I don't use it to overrule a safety veto or protect my planned story. If there is a conflict about a rule or a ruling, I bring it to a table vote and move on.

For ordinary rules questions, I use this loose framework:

1. **I don't know the rule:** The player can do it for now. I let them know I'll look it up for future sessions.
2. **The character probably can't do it, but the attempt is plausible:** They can do it, perhaps with a slight increase in difficulty.
3. **The action is too outlandish for the character:** I use “No, but.” I say no to that specific action, then discuss other ways the character could accomplish their goal.

If the dice are getting in the way of the table's fun, I set them aside and play make-believe instead. Rules and systems are tools; they don't outrank the people playing.
