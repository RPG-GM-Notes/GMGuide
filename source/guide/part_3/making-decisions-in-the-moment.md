<!-- (dl (section-meta 8. Making Decisions in the Moment)) -->

<!-- (dl (# Choosing How to Resolve Something)) -->

When a character tries something, I first decide whether the outcome needs resolving at all. If it is clear from the situation, I say what happens and keep play moving. If the outcome is uncertain and matters, I use the rules, make a ruling, or talk it through with the players. I choose the approach that helps us understand what happens without letting the process get in the way of the game.

Rules are useful tools, not authorities that outrank the people playing. If a rule supports the moment, I use it. If I do not know the rule, I let the player attempt the action for now and tell them I will check it for future sessions. If the dice or system are getting in the way of the table's fun, I set them aside and play make-believe instead.

<!-- (dl (# Handling Uncertainty and Missing Information)) -->

I do not need to know everything before making a decision. I use what is established: the Stage, the forces and their goals and limits, what the characters know, and what has already happened. If something important is unclear, I can ask the players what they mean, talk through the uncertainty, or make a provisional ruling so play can continue.

When I have to fill a gap, I want the answer to belong to the world we have established. I do not treat uncertainty as permission to invent a consequence that has no connection to the situation. If I later discover that I misunderstood a rule or a fact, I can correct how I handle it from then on without erasing the events that have already happened.

<!-- (dl (# Making Rulings Players Can Understand)) -->

I try to make rulings in terms of the character and the situation, and to say enough that the players understand what I am deciding. I do not need to turn every call into a long debate. My usual framework is:

1. **I do not know the rule:** The player can do it for now. I will look up the rule before a future session.
2. **The character probably cannot do it, but the attempt is plausible:** I may allow it with a slight increase in difficulty.
3. **The action is too outlandish for the character:** I use “No, but.” I say no to that specific action, then help the player find another way to pursue the character's goal.

If there is a conflict about a rule or ruling, I bring it to a table vote and move on. Safety is different: any player can veto content without explanation, and that veto is not put to a vote.

<!-- (dl (# Consistency Without Being Trapped by Precedent)) -->

I want events to fit the players' view of the world because that helps them trust it. Literal consistency is less important than that appearance: a new explanation or detail can be worked in afterward if it does not break what the players understand to be true. Players often help make those connections themselves. This is separate from rules precedent. Sometimes I make a call in the moment and later learn that the rules do not support it. What happened, happened. The character did the thing, and the consequences remain part of the game. It was a fluke; the same action is not necessarily possible again.

I explain the correction and apply the rules or ruling consistently going forward. I do not take back a past success just to make the record match the rulebook. This lets me correct course without making the players feel that their earlier choices or accomplishments can be retroactively invalidated.

<!-- (dl (# Correcting a Decision That Did Not Work)) -->

If a decision is making play less fun, undermining a character, or creating a problem I did not intend, I can acknowledge that and change how I handle things. I do not have to defend a bad call just because I made it. I explain what I am changing, preserve what has already happened where I can, and move forward with a clearer understanding.

There is one exception to leaving past events in place. If an action causes someone to raise a safety concern, that action is erased, along with its direct consequences. Safety overrides continuity, rules, and the desire to preserve what happened. I make a note of the concern without asking for an explanation, then help the affected player find another way forward if the action was theirs.
