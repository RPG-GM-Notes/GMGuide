<!-- (dl (# Choosing a System for the Experience I Want)) -->

For me, the system is almost inconsequential. You can tell any kind of story with any kind of system. What matters most is that the table will have fun with the system; everything after that can be patched.

That does not mean system choice is meaningless. I recommend that GMs understand what they like and dislike. I personally don't run tactical systems anymore because I don't find them fun. I prefer story-based systems, so I tend to run games such as World of Darkness, Fate, and Cypher. Those are my tastes, not a universal recommendation. Choose a system the table wants to play, then adapt what needs adapting.

<!-- (dl (# Finding Players Who Want That Experience)) -->

I am up front with players about the kind of game I expect to run. I may not reveal the exact theme if doing so would give too much away, but I talk about what kinds of things will be in the story and what parts of play are likely to take focus: combat, narrative, investigation, or some combination.

I also tell players what topics I want to address, especially when those topics might be trigger topics. Players need enough information to decide whether they want to join, even when some details of the story remain a surprise.

<!-- (dl (# Establishing Expectations, Boundaries, and Feedback)) -->

Safety is the ultimate authority at my table. We decide together what can initially go into the game and when it can happen. Every player can veto content at any time, no questions asked. If a veto is used, I remove the offending context, make a note, and after the session ask only whether I recorded it correctly so it can be added to our banned-topics list.

I don't start a game with a player until we have practiced vetoing topics twice. The first practice is usually silly: I might say, “Ham is your trigger topic and bread is yours,” then describe a scene that includes one of those topics so everyone can try using a veto. I repeat the exercise with a different, real example, such as animal abuse, if it is not already on the forbidden-topics list.

The practices help make vetoing an available action before a real boundary is tested in play. A veto does not require explanation. If the veto concerns an action by a player character, I work with that player to find another action the character can take.
